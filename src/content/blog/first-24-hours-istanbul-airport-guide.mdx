You've just landed in Istanbul. It's early morning, your check-in is hours away, and you're standing in the arrivals hall with a suitcase and a decision to make. This guide — the written companion to Episode 1 of our podcast *Istanbul Unpacked* — walks you through exactly how to turn a groggy arrival day into your favorite day of the trip.

## Step 1: Know Your Airport — IST vs. SAW

Istanbul has two international airports, and mixing them up is the most expensive mistake a first-timer can make.

**Istanbul Airport (IST)** sits on the European side, about 40 km from the historic center. It's one of the largest airports in the world — allow at least 20 minutes just to walk from your gate to the exit.

**Sabiha Gökçen (SAW)** is on the Asian side and mostly serves low-cost carriers. It's smaller, faster to exit, and surprisingly well connected since the metro extension opened.

### Getting into the city from IST

- **M11 Metro:** the cheapest and most traffic-proof way toward the center.
- **Havaist buses:** direct routes to Sultanahmet, Taksim, Kadıköy and more. Comfortable, luggage-friendly, roughly the price of a coffee and a sandwich.
- **Taxi:** expect around €30–40 to the center. Use the official taxi queue or a ride app only — politely ignore anyone offering you a "special price" inside the terminal.

### Getting into the city from SAW

- **M4 Metro:** connects the airport directly to Kadıköy — a genuine game changer for Asian-side stays.
- **Havabus shuttles:** direct to Taksim and Kadıköy.
- **Taxi:** reasonable to Asian-side neighborhoods; expensive if you're crossing to Europe.

**Rule of thumb:** if your hotel is in Sultanahmet, Karaköy or Taksim, fly into IST if you can. Staying in Kadıköy or Moda? SAW is actually more convenient.

## Step 2: Money, Data, and the Istanbulkart

Handle these three things before leaving the airport and everything else gets easier.

**Currency:** Turkey uses the Turkish lira (TRY). Skip the airport exchange counters — withdraw lira from a bank ATM or simply pay by card. Contactless payment is accepted almost everywhere in Istanbul, including most street food stalls.

**Mobile data:** if your phone supports eSIM, buy a Turkey data plan online before you fly — it typically costs a fraction of the airport SIM price. Physical SIMs are cheaper at official Turkcell or Vodafone stores in the city than at the airport.

**Istanbulkart:** this rechargeable card is your key to the entire city — metro, tram, bus, funicular and, best of all, the Bosphorus ferries. Buy one from the yellow machines at any station, top it up, and tap in. One card works for multiple travelers.

## Step 3: Solve the Luggage Problem (Before It Ruins Your Day)

Here's the situation nobody plans for: you land at 7 a.m., your hotel or Airbnb check-in is at 3 p.m., and you're facing eight hours with a heavy suitcase in a city famous for hills, cobblestones and crowded trams.

Your options:

1. **Wait at the hotel.** Some hotels store bags before check-in — but many boutique places have minimal space, and most Airbnb hosts can't hold luggage at all.
2. **Airport lockers.** Both airports offer left-luggage counters, but that means returning to the airport later — an expensive detour if your evening plans are in the city.
3. **Luggage delivery.** Services like [Keepsmybag](/luggage-delivery) let you hand your suitcase to a verified, insured courier at IST or SAW arrivals. Your bag is tracked with a QR code and delivered directly to your hotel or Airbnb, timed to your check-in. You walk out of the airport hands-free and start your trip immediately.

For travelers arriving early — especially Airbnb guests with strict check-in times — luggage delivery turns dead waiting hours into a free half-day in the city.

## Step 4: The Perfect Jet-Lag-Proof First Afternoon

Don't attempt Hagia Sophia on arrival day. The lines are long, your energy is short, and the Old City deserves better than a jet-lagged shuffle. Instead:

**Eat at a lokanta.** These casual restaurants display home-style Turkish dishes behind glass — just point at what looks good. Fast, affordable, authentic.

**Take a ferry across the Bosphorus.** The single best first-day activity in Istanbul. Board a public ferry from Eminönü or Karaköy to Kadıköy — it costs about a dollar with your Istanbulkart, and for twenty minutes you're crossing between two continents with a glass of tea in hand.

**Catch golden hour at Galata Bridge.** Fishermen along the railings, minarets against an orange sky, and balık ekmek (fish sandwich) boats smoking below. This is the Istanbul postcard, and it's free.

**Sleep early.** Istanbul rewards energy. Rest tonight — the Old City is tomorrow's adventure.

## Three Day-One Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Unofficial airport taxis.** If someone approaches you offering a ride, walk away. Official queues and apps only.
2. **Overplanning arrival day.** Museums and palaces will still be there tomorrow. Jet lag is real.
3. **Dragging your luggage "just for a few hours."** Istanbul's terrain is merciless with a suitcase. Send it ahead or store it — your back and your first impressions will thank you.
