Istanbul's best food isn't in its fanciest restaurants — it's on carts, in boats, behind steamed glass, and in shops that have sold one thing perfectly for a century. This self-guided crawl (the written companion to Episode 5 of our podcast *Istanbul Unpacked*) takes you through a full eating day, morning to midnight, across both continents — for around €30 total.

## The Rules of the Crawl

1. **Follow the local crowds.** A queue of office workers at a food cart is a Michelin star. An empty stall on a busy street is a review — trust it.
2. **One thing per stop.** Every stop below does one thing brilliantly. Order that, share if needed, keep moving.
3. **Carry some cash.** Cards work nearly everywhere, but small vendors move faster with small lira notes.

**Is Istanbul street food safe?** Generally yes — turnover is so fast that freshness takes care of itself, and the busy-stall rule is your insurance. Stick to bottled or filtered water.

## Morning: Simit, Börek, or the Full Turkish Breakfast

**Simit (08:00).** The sesame-crusted bread ring sold from red carts on nearly every corner — crisp outside, chewy inside, eaten walking, plain or with cream cheese. This is the city's heartbeat food; millions are sold daily.

**Börek (09:00).** Find a börekçi — a shop baking nothing but layered filo pastry with cheese, spinach or minced meat, sold by weight. The connoisseur's order is su böreği, the soft, almost lasagna-like version locals treat as the true test of the shop. Black tea alongside is not optional — assume tea at every stop in this guide.

**The alternative: serpme kahvaltı.** If you'd rather sit, the full Turkish breakfast covers the table in small plates — cheeses, olives, honey with clotted cream, eggs with spicy sucuk, warm bread. It's built for two or more and will erase your lunch plans. Choose this *or* the crawl morning, not both.

## Midday: The Waterfront Classics

**Balık ekmek at Eminönü (12:00).** Grilled mackerel with onions, lettuce and lemon in half a loaf of bread, eaten on a stool meters from the water, from the boats beside the Galata Bridge. A few euros; on the right day, a top-five travel meal anywhere.

**The sour sidekick:** vendors nearby sell şalgam — fermented purple-carrot juice, salty and sour, the traditional partner to the fish sandwich. First sip: confusion. Third sip: understanding.

**Islak burger in Taksim (13:30).** The "wet burger": a small beef burger whose bun is steamed in garlicky tomato sauce until it glistens, sold from steamy glass cabinets around Taksim Square. Two bites each. Nobody in recorded history has eaten exactly one.

## Afternoon: Sweet Interlude, Then the Asian Side

**Baklava + Turkish coffee (15:00).** Mid-afternoon is dessert o'clock. Order one or two pieces of pistachio baklava at an established baklava house — not a tray — with Turkish coffee (medium-sweet or unsweetened) to cut the syrup. Flip the finished cup onto its saucer; reading the grounds is a national pastime.

**Ferry to Kadıköy (16:00).** As covered in our [two-continents guide](/blog/european-vs-asian-side-istanbul-guide), the Asian side is where Istanbul really eats — and the twenty-minute ferry doubles as a digestif.

**The market graze (16:30).** Not one stop but a slow drift through Kadıköy's market streets: taste olives before buying, sample Turkish delight cut fresh from the block, accept the cup of pickle juice at the pickle shop (mandatory), and watch the fishmongers' theater. A pocket bag of roasted chickpeas or dried mulberries covers the walking stretches.

**For the brave (18:00):** two street classics with cult followings. Midye dolma — mussels stuffed with spiced rice, lemon squeezed over, eaten shell after shell until you lose count (they're priced per piece; the vendor keeps score). And kokoreç — seasoned grilled lamb intestines chopped with tomato and spices into bread. It sounds like a dare; it eats like a reward.

## Night: The Midnight Soup

Istanbul's food day doesn't end — it changes shifts. Late-night çorba (soup) restaurants stay open into the small hours serving lentil soup with lemon and chili flakes, or the legendary cure-all işkembe (tripe soup). A bright, steamy soup shop at 1 a.m. — full of taxi drivers, students and travelers — is one of the city's quiet perfections, and the correct final stop.
