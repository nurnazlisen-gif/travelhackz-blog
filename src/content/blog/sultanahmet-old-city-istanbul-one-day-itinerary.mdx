Every guidebook lists the same five sights in Istanbul's Old City. Almost none of them tell you the *order* — and the order is everything. This guide (the written companion to Episode 3 of our podcast *Istanbul Unpacked*) gives you a timed route that keeps you one step ahead of the tour-bus wave from morning to golden hour.

## The Strategy: Ride Ahead of the Wave

Crowds in Sultanahmet move predictably. Tour groups and cruise passengers arrive between 10 and 11 a.m., swallow Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque, then flood the Grand Bazaar after lunch. Your whole day is built on one principle: **be where the wave isn't.**

Three rules before you start:

1. **Start at 8 a.m.** One early alarm buys you a full day of walking into places others queue an hour for.
2. **Dress for mosques from the start.** Shoulders and knees covered for everyone; women need a head covering inside mosques (scarves are provided at entrances, but your own is more comfortable).
3. **Carry as little as possible.** The Old City punishes baggage — more on this below.

## Morning: Two Giants Before the Crowds

**Sultanahmet Square (8:00).** This was the Hippodrome of Constantinople — the arena of chariot races and imperial riots. The Egyptian Obelisk here is around 3,500 years old and looks freshly carved. Grab a Turkish tea and take it in while the square is quiet.

**The Blue Mosque (8:30).** Officially the Sultanahmet Mosque, and free to visit outside the five daily prayer times. The name comes from more than 20,000 handmade İznik tiles lining the interior. Shoes off at the entrance (bags provided), voices low.

**Hagia Sophia (9:15).** Built in 537 as the cathedral of Byzantium, later a mosque, then a museum, now a mosque again — the entire history of the city under one dome. This is the one line in Istanbul you can't fully avoid; arriving before the 10 a.m. wave is your only real weapon. When you enter, look up first. Everyone does.

**Basilica Cistern (10:30).** Two minutes' walk away: an underground forest of 336 columns rising from dark water, with two ancient Medusa heads reused as column bases — one sideways, one upside down, purpose unknown. Cool, quiet, slightly eerie, and done in 30 minutes.

## Midday: Eat Well, Then Choose Your Palace

**Lunch (11:30).** Skip the restaurants directly on the square — triple the price for average food. Walk five minutes toward Çemberlitaş and find an esnaf lokantası (tradesmen's restaurant), where dishes steam behind glass and you simply point.

**The decision (12:30).** Topkapı Palace — 400 years of Ottoman sultans, the Harem, the treasury, Bosphorus-view courtyards — is magnificent and takes a genuine 2.5–3 hours. If palaces aren't your thing, take the honest alternative: the **Süleymaniye Mosque**, fifteen minutes away. It's the masterpiece of the imperial architect Sinan, nearly tourist-free, and its terrace has the best Golden Horn view in the Old City. Free.

Choose one, not both. This is a day, not a triathlon.

## Afternoon: The Grand Bazaar, With an Exit Plan

By 3:30 p.m. the wave has reached the Bazaar — and honestly, it's better with a bit of chaos. Sixty-one streets, some four thousand shops, over five centuries of continuous commerce.

Three survival rules:

1. **Go sideways.** Main streets are for looking; the side alleys and the old hans (caravanserai courtyards) are where it gets interesting.
2. **Bargain the right things.** Haggling is expected for carpets, leather and jewelry — not for small souvenirs. Start around half the asking price, keep smiling, and be genuinely ready to walk away. Walking away is how the real price appears.
3. **Set an exit alarm.** The Bazaar is a labyrinth by design. People lose hours.

**Exit through the Spice Bazaar (17:00).** Walk downhill toward Eminönü and finish in the Spice Bazaar — smaller, denser, and fragrant with saffron, dried roses, Turkish delight and coffee. Buy your edible souvenirs here; prices beat the Grand Bazaar for the same goods.

## The Luggage Warning (Read This If It's Your Arrival or Departure Day)

The Old City was built for camels and porters, not roller bags. Cobblestones, hills, packed tram cars and shoulder-to-shoulder bazaar alleys make Sultanahmet the single worst place in Istanbul to be carrying luggage — and a backpack in a dense crowd invites exactly the wrong kind of attention.

If your Old City day falls on arrival or departure day, don't bring your bags anywhere near it. Options:

## Golden Hour Finish

End at the **Galata Bridge** around sunset: fishermen along the railings, the New Mosque glowing gold, fish-sandwich boats smoking below. If you have one more hour, board any public ferry and watch the Old City skyline — domes and minarets stacked on the hill — slide past from the water for about a dollar.
