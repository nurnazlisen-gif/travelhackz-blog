Bodrum gets filed under "Turkish Riviera nightlife," which undersells it. The peninsula is a scatter of genuinely different bays — some built for beach clubs and boat parties, others quiet enough that the loudest thing around is a call to prayer — connected by a single old town with a 15th-century castle sitting directly on the harbor. This guide is about picking the right piece of it.

## Bodrum Town: The Castle and the Old Town

**Bodrum Castle (Castle of St Peter).** Built by the Knights Hospitaller in the early 1400s using stone partly salvaged from the ancient Mausoleum at Halicarnassus, the castle dominates the harbor and now houses the **Museum of Underwater Archaeology** — shipwreck cargo, ancient glassware and one of the best collections of its kind anywhere, all inside a genuinely striking medieval fortress. Budget at least 90 minutes.

**The old town.** White-washed buildings, bougainvillea, narrow lanes running down to two marinas — the eastern marina for the bar-and-restaurant strip, the western one quieter and more local. It's compact enough to walk entirely in an afternoon.

**The Mausoleum at Halicarnassus.** One of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, and — honestly — one of the least visually impressive today, since most of its stone was reused to build the castle centuries ago. Worth a quick stop for the history if you're nearby; not worth a special trip on its own.

**A boat trip.** The single best way to see the coastline here — a day on a traditional wooden gulet, stopping at coves and swimming spots along the peninsula, is close to the default Bodrum experience for a reason.

## Which Bay Should You Actually Stay In?

The peninsula is small in distance but genuinely varied in character. Base yourself by matching the bay to what you actually want:

| Bay | Character | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Bodrum town | Nightlife, castle, marina, restaurants | First-timers, short stays, no rental car |
| Gümbet | Budget resorts, lively beach clubs | Younger crowds, tight budgets |
| Türkbükü | Upscale beach clubs, yacht scene | A see-and-be-seen weekend |
| Yalıkavak | Marina, boutique shopping, calmer | A quieter, still-polished base |
| Gümüşlük | Fishing village, sunset views, low-key | Travelers who want Bodrum without the party |

**The honest trade-off:** the quieter, more scenic bays (Gümüşlük especially) sit 30–40 minutes from Bodrum town's nightlife and castle. If you want both worlds in one trip, consider splitting a week between town and one peninsula bay rather than committing to just one base.

## Getting Around

Bodrum town is walkable on its own, but the peninsula's bays are spread across a genuinely large area — a **rental car or scooter** is the practical choice if you're staying outside town and want to explore more than one bay. **Dolmuş** minibuses run the main routes on a loose schedule and are cheap; taxis work but add up quickly if you're commuting into town daily from a quieter bay.

## Three Bodrum Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Booking a hotel in a party bay if you actually want quiet, or vice versa.** The bays genuinely differ in character — check reviews for noise and crowd, not just beach photos.
2. **Skipping the castle for the beach.** It's one of the best-preserved Crusader-era fortresses on the Turkish coast, and it's right in the middle of town — easy to fold into any day.
3. **Underestimating peninsula distances.** "Bodrum" on a map looks small; a bay-to-bay drive with summer traffic can easily eat 40–60 minutes each way.
