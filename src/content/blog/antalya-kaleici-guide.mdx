Antalya's coastline is lined with resort hotels, and most visitors never see anything else. That's a shame, because the city has an old town — Kaleiçi — with Roman gates, Ottoman merchant houses and a working harbor that predates the beach clubs by about two thousand years. This guide covers a day spent entirely inside it.

## What Kaleiçi Actually Is

Kaleiçi ("inside the castle") is Antalya's old walled city, a tight maze of narrow streets wrapped around a small Roman-era harbor. It survived as a working neighborhood through Roman, Byzantine, Seljuk and Ottoman rule, which is why the architecture layers so oddly and so well — a Roman gate here, an Ottoman wooden house there, a Seljuk minaret a block over. Most of the resort development that made Antalya famous sits well outside these walls.

## The Day, Roughly in Order

**Start at Hadrian's Gate.** A triple-arched Roman gate built in 130 AD for Emperor Hadrian's visit, and still the ceremonial entrance into the old town. The marble columns and arches are original — an unusually intact piece of Roman civic architecture to just walk straight through on your way to breakfast.

**Wander the old streets, no fixed route.** Kaleiçi rewards aimlessness more than most old towns — Ottoman-era houses with overhanging upper floors, small courtyards turned into cafés, the odd Byzantine church converted to a mosque and back again. The narrow, largely pedestrian streets make this genuinely comfortable to just wander without a plan for an hour or two.

**Yivli Minare (the Fluted Minaret).** A distinctive brick minaret from the Seljuk period, visible from much of the old town and one of the city's oldest surviving Islamic monuments — a useful landmark to reorient by when you inevitably get turned around.

**The old harbor.** The reason Kaleiçi exists at all: a small, sheltered Roman-era harbor, now lined with cafés and used mostly by tour boats and yachts. Walk down to the water for the view back up at the old city walls, or take a short boat trip along the coast if you want to see Antalya's cliffside setting from the water.

**Antalya Museum (if you have the afternoon).** A short walk or taxi ride from Kaleiçi, this is one of Turkey's best archaeological museums — statues and mosaics from the nearby ancient cities of Perge and Aspendos, laid out well enough to be worth the detour even if you're not a museum person.

**Sunset at the harbor or the clifftop park.** Karaalioğlu Park, just south of the old town, sits on cliffs above the sea with views along the coast — a quieter, more local spot for sunset than the harbor itself.

## Using Antalya as a Base

Antalya's location makes it a reasonable hub for a few well-known day trips, if you have more than one day:

- **Aspendos:** an extraordinarily well-preserved Roman theater, still used for performances, about an hour east.
- **Perge:** an extensive Roman-era city ruin with a stadium and colonnaded street, closer to the city and easily combined with Aspendos.
- **Kaş and Kalkan:** smaller, quieter coastal towns west along the Mediterranean, popular for a slower pace and better swimming than Antalya's city beaches — a longer day trip, better as an overnight if you can manage it.

## Three Antalya Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Staying entirely at a resort outside the city.** It's a legitimate way to have a beach holiday, but it means never seeing the reason Antalya has been a real city for two thousand years.
2. **Trying to "do" Kaleiçi with a checklist.** The old town rewards wandering more than ticking off sights — leave slack in your plan.
3. **Underestimating the heat in summer.** Midday sun in Kaleiçi's stone streets is intense; plan the harbor and clifftop park for morning or evening, and save the museum's air conditioning for the hottest hours.
